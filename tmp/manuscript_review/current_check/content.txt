P0088	[Normal]	INTRODUCTION
P0089	[Normal]	Background of the Study
P0090	[Normal]	Agriculture plays an important role in food supply, livelihood, and local economic activity. In agricultural communities, farmers and decision-makers rely on timely and organized information to support crop planning, production monitoring, and response to crop-related problems. However, agricultural information is often recorded separately by farmers, local offices, and administrative agencies, which can make it difficult to produce updated and reliable data for planning. This problem becomes more significant in vegetable-producing areas where crops are seasonal, affected by weather conditions, and sensitive to delays in planting, harvesting, and distribution.
P0091	[Normal]	The need for digital support in agriculture is connected to the broader modernization of the Philippine agricultural sector. The World Bank (2020) emphasized that transforming Philippine agriculture into a more dynamic and diversified sector is important not only for food security but also for improving the welfare of rural communities. This supports the need for agricultural systems that can help organize production data, improve monitoring, and provide useful information for both farmers and government decision-makers.
P0092	[Normal]	The Philippine Development Plan 2023-2028 also emphasizes the modernization of agriculture and agribusiness. The plan views agriculture as part of a larger agri-food system that includes production, processing, marketing, consumption, and waste management. It also highlights the need to improve agricultural efficiency, strengthen value chains, and support the adoption of improved technologies. This relates to Harviana because the system contributes to agricultural modernization by organizing crop records, improving monitoring, and supporting data-based decision-making among farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators.
P0093	[Normal]	The Department of Agriculture’s National Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization and Industrialization Plan 2021-2030 further supports the need for coordinated and technology-assisted agricultural development. The Agricultural Training Institute (2022) described NAFMIP as a whole-of-nation plan that guides sector-wide growth and encourages public and private stakeholders to work toward a shared agricultural vision. This is connected to Harviana because the system provides a digital workflow that links farm-level records with validation and administrative monitoring, allowing agricultural information to move in a more organized and coordinated manner.
P0094	[Normal]	In the Philippines, the use of digital technology in agriculture has become increasingly important. Briones, Galang, and Latigar (2023) discussed that digital agriculture can support productivity, market access, sustainability, and decision-support systems. However, they also noted that digital agriculture must address issues such as the digital divide, rural connectivity, and the need to harmonize government data and advisory services. This shows that digital tools should not only collect data but should also organize information in a way that can be used by farmers and agricultural offices.
P0095	[Normal]	The Food and Agriculture Organization (2022) also emphasized that automation and digital technologies can contribute to more resilient and productive agrifood systems. The report explained that agricultural automation can improve productivity, resource-use efficiency, and sustainability when properly adopted. However, it also recognized that small-scale producers may face barriers in accessing and using these technologies. This supports the need for agricultural systems that are practical and accessible to local users. Harviana responds to this concern by providing a web-based system that focuses on organized crop planning, reporting, validation, and administrative monitoring rather than requiring complex equipment from farmers.
P0096	[Normal]	The use of information and communication technologies in agriculture is also supported by the E-Agriculture Strategy Guide developed by the Food and Agriculture Organization and the International Telecommunication Union. FAO and ITU (2016) explained that e-agriculture strategies help countries use ICT in a more organized way by establishing a vision, action plan, and monitoring framework. This supports the need for systems like Harviana, which apply digital tools to improve agricultural data collection, monitoring, reporting, and decision support.
P0097	[Normal]	At the policy level, digital technologies can also improve how agricultural programs are planned and implemented. The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (2019) explained that digital innovations can help agricultural policies by reducing information gaps, lowering transaction costs, and improving the way different actors work together. This is relevant to Harviana because the system connects farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators in one workflow. Through this structure, the information submitted by farmers can be reviewed, organized, and used by administrators for reports and supply-related monitoring.
P0098	[Normal]	At the system level, digital agriculture becomes more useful when data, users, and decision-making tools are connected in one organized structure. The World Bank, Gates Foundation, and Boston Consulting Group (2025) described a Digital Agriculture Roadmap as a strategy and implementation plan that guides the transformation of agriculture through digital technologies. The report also emphasized that digital agriculture involves user-facing applications, data systems, integration layers, and decision-support tools. This is related to Harviana because the system connects farmer-submitted crop plans, harvest records, damage reports, LGU validation, and DA administrative monitoring into a single digital workflow.
P0099	[Normal]	The growing discussion on digital agriculture in the Philippines also shows the importance of preparing farmers and agricultural offices for technology-based systems. The International Rice Research Institute and CGIAR (2024) reported that Philippine stakeholders have recognized digital technologies as an important part of the future of agriculture, while also noting challenges such as digital literacy, infrastructure limitations, and data security. These concerns are important in developing agricultural systems because technology must be usable, secure, and meaningful to its target users. Harviana addresses this by providing role-based access for farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators, allowing each user group to access only the functions related to their responsibilities.
P0100	[Normal]	The Cordillera Administrative Region, particularly Benguet, is one of the country’s major sources of highland vegetables. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority RSSO-CAR (2025), CAR produced 432,068.1 metric tons of vegetables in 2024, with Benguet contributing 371,151.5 metric tons or 85.9 percent of the region’s total vegetable production. Because of Benguet’s major role in vegetable supply, crop production records, damage reports, harvest information, and planting schedules are important for monitoring agricultural conditions. Aquino, Delos Reyes, and Catelo (2025) also emphasized the importance of Benguet-sourced vegetables in food value chain networks, showing how vegetable production in the province contributes to household food access and wider supply movement.
P0101	[Normal]	Despite this importance, farmers and agricultural offices may still experience difficulties in monitoring crop activities, validating field reports, and converting submitted farm information into useful administrative records. Farmers need a system that can help them plan crops, monitor farm activities, report damages, and record actual harvest results. Local government validators need a way to review submitted information before it is used for decision-making. DA administrators, on the other hand, need consolidated records that can support monitoring, reporting, and forecasting. Without an organized system, agricultural information may become delayed, incomplete, or difficult to verify.
P0102	[Normal]	Agricultural extension and advisory services also play an important role in helping farmers and agricultural offices use information for better decisions. The Philippine Agriculture and Fisheries Extension Strategic Plan 2023-2028 emphasizes the need to reframe extension and advisory services for food systems transformation. The Agricultural Training Institute (2023) stated that the plan supports efficient production, competitiveness, stronger extension institutions, and empowered farmers and fisherfolk. Harviana supports this direction by providing farmers and agricultural administrators with a platform where crop plans, damage reports, harvest records, and validation results can be accessed and used for monitoring.
P0103	[Normal]	The Agricultural Training Institute’s e-Extension Program also shows how digital systems can support agricultural information delivery in the Philippines. ATI describes e-Extension as the digital strategy of the Department of Agriculture for delivering agricultural extension services. It provides farmers, fisherfolk, agricultural extension workers, and other stakeholders with access to agriculture and fisheries-related information, online courses, and technical support. This strengthens the relevance of Harviana because the system also uses a digital platform to support farmers and agricultural offices, particularly in organizing crop-related records and making information easier to access.
P0104	[Normal]	Weather conditions also affect agricultural production. PAGASA describes the Philippines as having a tropical and maritime climate characterized by relatively high temperature, high humidity, and abundant rainfall. These conditions make weather-related monitoring important for crop planning and farm activity scheduling. For farmers, access to crop calendars and weather information can help them become more aware of possible risks. For administrators, weather and location-based information can support better interpretation of crop conditions and production records.
P0105	[Normal]	Digital agriculture is also useful in addressing agricultural risks related to changing environmental conditions. Stephenson et al. (2021) explained that digital agriculture can support climate adaptation and that spatial information can help inform government priorities and decisions related to crop suitability and agricultural planning. This supports Harviana’s use of map-based monitoring and weather information, since these features help users relate crop records with location and field conditions without requiring manual consolidation of data.
P0106	[Normal]	GIS and WebGIS technologies have become important in agricultural monitoring because they allow production records to be viewed and analyzed according to location. Raihan (2024) emphasized that Geographic Information Systems can support evidence-based agricultural decision-making by helping users analyze spatial information related to crop production, sustainability, and planning. Similarly, Cui et al. (2022) demonstrated the use of open-source WebGIS tools in managing agricultural land information, while Patel et al. (2023) showed how Web GIS-based decision-support systems can assist in agricultural monitoring and management. These studies support the development of Harviana as a GIS-integrated system that visualizes vegetable production records through map-based monitoring.
P0107	[Normal]	Recent studies also show the value of combining agricultural data, forecasting, and spatial monitoring for better production-related decisions. Alemán-Montes et al. (2025) presented a near real-time spatial decision-support system that used a satellite mapping web browser to support crop monitoring. Wu et al. (2023) also emphasized that reliable crop monitoring requires timely and useful crop information that can support food security decisions. In relation to crop forecasting, Ali et al. (2022) discussed the role of multi-sensor remote sensing in crop yield prediction, while Jabed and Azmi Murad (2024) reviewed how machine learning and deep learning approaches can support crop yield prediction. These studies support Harviana’s use of map visualization, production estimation, and forecasting as decision-support features.
P0108	[Normal]	Local studies further strengthen the relevance of Harviana to vegetable production in Benguet. Pilay and Werdenberg (2024) applied SARIMA-based forecasting to vegetable production in Benguet, showing the importance of analyzing production trends to understand possible future outputs. Quitaleg and Dumlao (2023) also developed a decision-support system for Benguet upland vegetable crop prediction using machine learning techniques. These studies show that forecasting and decision-support systems are relevant to Benguet’s vegetable production context. Harviana builds on this direction by combining crop planning, farmer-submitted records, LGU validation, GIS visualization, supply forecasting, and DA report generation in one web-based platform.
P0109	[Normal]	In response to these needs, this study proposes Harviana, a web-based agricultural decision-support system designed to assist farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators. Harviana allows farmers to create crop plans, view calendar-based farm activities, receive ML-based production estimates, submit crop damage reports, and record actual harvest results. Submitted reports can be reviewed by LGU validators to improve the reliability of information before it is used for administrative monitoring. DA administrators can view dashboards, monitor crop records, access interactive map and weather information, generate reports, and review real-time supply forecasts.
P0110	[Normal]	Harviana is designed to create a connected flow of agricultural information from farmers to LGU validators and DA administrators. Through this structure, the system helps transform farmer-submitted data into validated and organized records that can support crop monitoring, report generation, and supply-related decision-making. The system supports the goals of digital agriculture by improving the collection, validation, visualization, and use of agricultural data.
P0111	[Normal]	The study is also aligned with Sustainable Development Goal 2: Zero Hunger, which promotes sustainable agriculture, improved food security, and support for small-scale farmers. Harviana contributes to this goal by helping improve crop planning, harvest monitoring, and agricultural reporting. It also supports Sustainable Development Goal 12, particularly the reduction of food losses along production and supply chains, by allowing the system to record damage reports, actual harvests, and production-related information that can support better agricultural monitoring.

P0113	[Normal]	This study is significant because it provides a digital approach to improving how agricultural information is collected, validated, monitored, and used for decision-making. Harviana supports the flow of crop-related data from farmers to LGU validators and DA administrators, allowing crop plans, damage reports, actual harvest records, and supply-related information to be organized in one system. Through this, the study contributes to the improvement of agricultural data management, crop monitoring, report generation, and decision-support processes.

P0126	[Normal]	Definition of Terms
P0127	[Normal]	To ensure clarity and consistency throughout this study, the following terms are defined based on how they are used in the development and discussion of Harviana.
P0128	[Normal]	Actual Harvest Record. This refers to the record submitted by a farmer after harvesting a crop. In this study, it includes the harvest amount, unit, harvest date, photo evidence, and notes. The system uses this record to compare expected production with the farmer-recorded harvest result.
P0129	[Normal]	Agricultural Decision-Support System. This refers to a digital system that helps users collect, organize, analyze, and present agricultural information for planning and decision-making. In this study, it refers to Harviana, which supports farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators in crop planning, validation, monitoring, forecasting, and reporting.
P0130	[Normal]	Crop Damage Report. This refers to a report submitted by a farmer when a crop has been affected by damage. In this study, it includes the cause of damage, affected crop plan, damaged area, damage date, optional notes, and photo evidence. The report is reviewed by the LGU validator before being used for monitoring and report generation.
P0131	[Normal]	Crop Plan. This refers to the farmer’s planned crop production record. In this study, it includes details such as crop type, planting information, farm area, expected harvest schedule, and related crop activities. Crop plans are used in the farmer calendar, ML-based production estimate, and supply forecast features of Harviana.
P0132	[Normal]	DA Administrator. This refers to the system user responsible for administrative monitoring of crop records, forecasts, reports, users, and system data. In this study, the DA administrator can access dashboards, map and weather data, supply forecasting, and report generation features.
P0133	[Normal]	Farmer. This refers to the primary user of Harviana who creates crop plans, views calendar activities, receives production estimates, submits crop damage reports, and records actual harvest results.
P0134	[Normal]	Farmer Calendar. This refers to the calendar feature of Harviana that displays crop plans, farm activities, reminders, and schedules. In this study, it helps farmers monitor planting, maintenance, and harvest-related activities.
P0135	[Normal]	Geographic Information System. This refers to a system used to store, manage, and display location-based information. In this study, GIS-related features are represented through Harviana’s interactive map, which helps users view agricultural records and weather information based on location.
P0136	[Normal]	Harviana. This refers to the proposed web-based agricultural decision-support system developed in this study. It supports crop planning, farmer calendar management, ML-based production estimation, crop damage reporting, actual harvest recording, LGU validation, DA monitoring, map and weather viewing, supply forecasting, and report generation.
P0137	[Normal]	LGU Validator. This refers to the system user responsible for reviewing and validating farmer-submitted crop damage reports. In this study, the LGU validator can approve reports or return them for correction.
P0138	[Normal]	Machine Learning. This refers to the use of computational models that learn from data to provide predictions or estimates. In this study, machine learning is used to support production estimation during crop planning and supply forecasting.
P0139	[Normal]	ML-Based Production Estimate. This refers to the estimated crop production generated by the system during the crop planning process. In this study, it gives farmers an expected production value based on the information provided in the crop plan and the system’s prediction support.
P0140	[Normal]	Real-Time Supply Forecast. This refers to the DA feature that provides a forecast of crop supply using available crop plan, harvest, damage, and validation-related data. In this study, it helps DA administrators review possible supply conditions for monitoring and planning.
P0141	[Normal]	Report Generation. This refers to the process of organizing system records into report-focused views that can be used for documentation, analysis, and administrative decision-making. In this study, DA administrators can generate and export planting and harvest-related reports.
P0142	[Normal]	Role-Based Access Control. This refers to a system security approach that limits access based on the role assigned to each user. In this study, Harviana provides different access and functions for farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators.
P0143	[Normal]	Usability. This refers to the extent to which users can use the system effectively, efficiently, and satisfactorily. In this study, usability is measured through usability testing conducted with farmers and DA administrators.
P0144	[Normal]	Web-Based System. This refers to a system that can be accessed through a web browser using an internet-connected device. In this study, Harviana is a web-based system designed for farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators.

P0149	[Normal]	METHODOLOGY
P0150	[Normal]	This chapter presents the methodology used in the development of Harviana. It discusses the software development methodology, design thinking process, scope and delimitation, data gathering techniques, sources of data, and software development tools used by the researchers. These methods served as the guide in identifying the needs of the users, designing the system features, developing the web-based platform, and evaluating the usability of Harviana.
P0151	[Normal]	Software Development Methodology
P0152	[Normal]	The researchers used the Agile Software Development Methodology in developing Harviana. Agile is an iterative approach to software development that allows the system to be built, tested, reviewed, and improved in repeated cycles. This methodology was selected because Harviana required continuous refinement based on the needs of different user groups, including farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators.
P0153	[Normal]	Agile was suitable for the development of Harviana because the system includes several connected features such as crop planning, farmer calendar management, ML-based production estimation, crop damage reporting, actual harvest recording, LGU validation, DA monitoring, map and weather viewing, supply forecasting, and report generation. Through Agile, the researchers were able to develop these features in stages, test them, and improve them based on feedback and system requirements.
P0154	[Normal]	The development of Harviana followed the main phases of Agile methodology: requirements gathering, design, development, testing, deployment, and review. These phases helped the researchers organize the development process and ensure that each part of the system supported the overall goal of providing an agricultural decision-support system. Figure 1 shows the Agile Software Development Methodology used in the development of Harviana.

P0159	[Normal]	Requirements Gathering. In this phase, the researchers identified the needs and concerns of the target users. The researchers focused on understanding the problems experienced by farmers in crop planning and farm monitoring, the need of LGU validators to review submitted reports, and the need of DA administrators to access organized agricultural data for monitoring and decision-making. The information gathered during this phase served as the basis for identifying the system requirements and core features of Harviana.
P0160	[Normal]	Design. In this phase, the researchers prepared the structure and interface design of the system. The design process included planning the user roles, system flow, database structure, and main interfaces for farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators. The researchers also created visual representations such as diagrams, wireframes, and prototypes to guide the development of the system. This phase helped ensure that the system would be understandable, role-based, and aligned with the needs of its users.
P0161	[Normal]	Development. In this phase, the researchers implemented the features of Harviana based on the approved design and system requirements. The system was developed as a web-based application with separate interfaces for farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators. Features such as crop planning, farmer calendar, production estimate, damage reporting, actual harvest recording, validation, dashboards, interactive map, weather viewing, supply forecasting, and report generation were developed and connected to the system database.
P0162	[Normal]	Testing. In this phase, the researchers tested the system to identify errors, check feature functionality, and determine whether the system met the intended requirements. The researchers tested the different user workflows, including farmer crop planning, report submission, LGU validation, DA dashboard monitoring, map viewing, and report generation. Testing helped the researchers identify issues that needed correction and ensured that the system functions worked properly before deployment and usability evaluation.
P0163	[Normal]	Deployment. In this phase, Harviana was prepared and deployed so that users could access the system through a web browser. The deployment phase allowed the researchers to test the system in a more realistic environment and confirm whether the system could support its intended users. This phase also helped ensure that the system database, machine learning support, and main web features were connected and accessible.
P0164	[Normal]	Review. In this phase, the researchers reviewed the system based on testing results, user feedback, and usability evaluation. The review phase allowed the researchers to identify areas for improvement and refine the system features. Through this process, Harviana was improved to better support crop planning, agricultural monitoring, report validation, and decision-making.
P0165	[Normal]	Design Thinking
P0166	[Normal]	Design Thinking was used by the researchers to understand the needs, problems, and expectations of the target users of Harviana. This approach helped the researchers focus on the actual experiences of farmers, LGU validators, and DA administrators before finalizing the system features. Since Harviana is intended to support agricultural planning, validation, monitoring, and decision-making, the researchers used Design Thinking to ensure that the system was based on real user concerns rather than assumptions.
P0167	[Normal]	Design Thinking is a human-centered and iterative process that allows developers to study user needs, define problems, generate possible solutions, create prototypes, and test the proposed system. In this study, the researchers followed the five phases of Design Thinking: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test. These phases guided the researchers in identifying the problems in agricultural data collection and monitoring, designing possible solutions, and improving Harviana based on user needs and feedback. Figure 2 shows the Design Thinking process used in the development of Harviana.

P0171	[Normal]	Empathize. The Empathize phase focused on understanding the experiences and challenges of the target users considered in the design of Harviana. The researchers gathered information from farmers and DA administrators to identify the difficulties they encounter in crop planning, record management, monitoring, reporting, and agricultural decision-making. The LGU Validator role was also considered in the system workflow because it serves as the validation layer between farmer-submitted records and DA administrative monitoring.
P0172	[Normal]	Based on the gathered information, farmers experienced challenges in organizing crop plans, monitoring farm activities, reporting crop damage, and keeping harvest records in a way that can be reviewed and used later. DA administrators needed consolidated and updated agricultural records that could support dashboards, map-based monitoring, supply forecasting, and report generation. Meanwhile, the LGU Validator role was identified as necessary for reviewing farmer-submitted reports and checking whether the information provided is acceptable before it becomes part of the system’s validated records. These findings helped the researchers identify the user needs that Harviana should address.

P0181	[Normal]	Define. The Define phase organized the information gathered during the Empathize phase into clear problem statements. The researchers identified that agricultural information is often recorded manually, stored separately, or difficult to validate and consolidate. Farmers need support in organizing crop plans, monitoring farm activities, submitting crop damage reports, and recording actual harvest results. DA administrators need reliable and updated agricultural data that can be used for dashboards, map-based monitoring, supply forecasting, and report generation.
P0182	[Normal]	Through this phase, the researchers defined the main problem as the lack of an integrated digital system that connects farmer-submitted agricultural records with validation and administrative monitoring. This problem affects the availability, reliability, and usability of crop-related information for planning and decision-making.
P0183	[Normal]	The researchers used the 5 Whys Analysis to identify the root causes of the problems encountered in agricultural record management and monitoring. This helped the researchers trace visible problems, such as scattered records, delayed reports, and difficulty in reviewing crop-related information, to deeper causes such as the lack of coordinated data flow, limited validation process, fragmented agricultural data, and absence of an integrated monitoring system.

P0252	[Normal]	Harviana is intended for three main user groups: farmers, LGU Validators, and DA Administrators. These features allow farmers to organize crop activities and submit crop-related information through a web-based platform.

P0254	[Normal]	For DA Administrators, Harviana provides features for dashboard monitoring, crop data viewing, interactive map and weather viewing, real-time supply forecast review, report generation, and export of agricultural records. These features are designed to support the monitoring of validated crop information and the preparation of agricultural reports for documentation and decision-making.
P0255	[Normal]	The study is delimited to vegetable production-related records within Benguet. The system does not cover agricultural records from other provinces or farming sectors outside the identified scope of the study. The production estimates and supply forecasts generated by Harviana are intended to serve as decision-support information and should not be treated as final or absolute production values. The reliability of these outputs depends on the completeness and accuracy of the data available in the system.
P0256	[Normal]	Harviana is developed as a web-based system that can be accessed through a browser using an internet-connected device. Instead, it provides digital support for crop planning, validation, visualization, forecasting, and reporting.

P0451	[Body Text]	Extent of the Usability of Harviana
P0452	[Body Text]	The extent of usability of Harviana was evaluated to determine how the system was perceived by its intended users after interacting with its major features. The evaluation focused on two user groups: farmers and DA Administrators. The farmer-side evaluation measured the usability of features such as crop planning, farmer calendar monitoring, damage report submission, and actual harvest recording. The DA Administrator-side evaluation measured the usability of features such as dashboard monitoring, interactive map and weather viewing, supply forecast review, report generation, and export of agricultural records.
P0453	[Body Text]	The researchers used two usability instruments to evaluate the system. The System Usability Scale (SUS) was used for the farmer respondents, while the USE questionnaire was used for the DA Administrator respondents. A total of 132 valid responses were used for the farmer-side SUS evaluation, while 18 valid responses were used for the DA Administrator-side USE evaluation. These responses were analyzed according to the scoring procedures and interpretation scales of the selected usability instruments.
P0454	[Body Text]	For the farmer evaluation, the System Usability Scale (SUS) developed by Brooke (1996) was used to measure the perceived usability of the farmer-side features of Harviana. The SUS is composed of ten statements rated using a five-point scale. For the computation, the odd-numbered items were scored by subtracting one from the participant’s response, while the even-numbered items were scored by subtracting the participant’s response from five. The adjusted scores were added together and multiplied by 2.5 to convert the result into a score ranging from 0 to 100. The interpretation of SUS scores was adapted from Bangor, Kortum, and Miller (2009).
