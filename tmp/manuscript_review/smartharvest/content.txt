7. Abstracts: 
7.1 Rationale/Background of the Study 
The agricultural sector in the Cordillera 
Administrative Region, particularly in Benguet, is highly 
vulnerable to erratic climate patterns, rendering 
traditional, intuition-based farming methods increasingly 
inadequate and prone to suboptimal yields. To address the 
critical need for data-driven agricultural management, 
the researchers developed PASYA, a unified Decision 
Support System (DSS) that integrates regional crop 
mapping with the SmartHarvest predictive engine. By 
leveraging the Random Forest machine learning algorithm 
to analyze historical crop yields alongside shifting 

===== PDF PAGE 4 =====

 
 
 ABSTRACT 4 
 
 
 
 
climate variables, the system forecasts optimal planting 
schedules, empowering local farmers and the Department of 
Agriculture (DA-CAR) to proactively mitigate risks, 
maximize resource efficiency, and modernize the regional 
agricultural supply chain. 
7.2 Summary 
This proposed study aims to design and develop 
PASYA: SmartHarvest, a dual-platform decision support 
system (DSS) aimed at optimizing agricultural 
productivity by connecting local farmers with data-driven 
crop planting schedules while providing the Department of 
Agriculture (DA-CAR) with regional supply monitoring 
tools. Specifically, the researchers aimed to achieve the 
following objectives: 
1. to identify the information requirements of the 
proposed system; 
2.  to determine the architectural framework of the 
proposed system; 
3. to identify the features of the proposed system; 
and 
4. to measure the extent of usability of the 
proposed system. 
The researchers selected the Agile Development 
methodology due to its iterative nature, flexibility, and 
strong emphasis on continuous stakeholder feedback. It 
consists of six main phases—requirements gathering, 
design, development, testing, deployment, and review—
allowing the team to simultaneously build the mobile 
application for the farmers and the web dashboard for 
administrators. Its collaborative framework ensured that 
complex adjustments to the machine learning forecasting 
models could be tested and refined in real-time alongside 
user interface improvements. 
 
7.3 Findings  
Based on the data gathered from the study’s 
objectives and overall analysis, the researchers 
identified the following findings: 
1.  The system’s requirements included key 
agricultural information such as farmer profiles, 
historical crop yields, and climate variables and 
technical specifications like a PHP (Laravel) framework, 

===== PDF PAGE 5 =====

 
 
 ABSTRACT 5 
 
 
 
 
Python machine learning engine, MySQL database, and 
internet connectivity. 
2.  The study utilized the 4+1 architectural 
framework to define the system’s structure, employing UML 
diagrams such as logical, development, process, physical 
views, and scenarios to clearly represent system 
components, user roles, and interactions. 
3.  The key features of SmartHarvest include Random 
Forest-Based Crop Schedule Forecasting, Regional Harvest 
Mapping, Farmer Production Logging, and the 
Administrative Monitoring Dashboard. 
4.  The application’s average SUS score of 71.55 for 
the farmer group falls within the ‘C’ grade range, 
indicating a ‘Good’ usability rating, while the USE 
Questionnaire mean of 6.32 for the DA-CAR staff indicates 
a ‘Strongly Agree’ rating. These scores place the mobile 
application in the 60th percentile, suggesting it 
performs better than over half of the systems evaluated 
using the same method. 
 
7.4. Conclusions 
Based on the data gathered, system development, and 
the overall findings of the study, the researchers have 
drawn the following conclusions: 
1.  The information requirements of the system were 
successfully identified and categorized into three main 
parts: farmers, DA administrators, and super 
administrators. Farmers must provide their full name, 
contact details, and a valid RSBSA number, while 
administrators need official credentials and contact 
details. Additionally, essential system requirements such 
as RAM, storage, OS version, and internet connectivity 
were successfully specified. 
2.  The structural design of the system was 
effectively guided by the 4+1 architectural framework. By 
utilizing this framework supported by UML diagrams, the 
researchers facilitated a smooth transition from the 
planning stages directly into system development. 
3.  The study successfully identified the core 
features necessary for the system to function as a 
decision support tool. These features include Random 
Forest-based crop schedule forecasting, comprehensive 

===== PDF PAGE 6 =====

 
 
 ABSTRACT 6 
 
 
 
 
yield analysis, agricultural weather forecasts, and a 
provincial monitoring dashboard. 
4. The SmartHarvest system is highly effective 
overall, though minor areas can still be improved. 
Usability testing showed that the admin dashboard earned 
a “Strongly Agree” rating from DA Staff with an overall 
weighted mean of 6.32 on the USE scale. Furthermore, the 
system placed within the ‘C- to C+’ (“OK-Good” / 
Marginal-Acceptable) grade range when evaluated by 
farmers utilizing the System Usability Scale (SUS). 
 
 
7.5 Recommendations 
From the conclusions drawn from the evaluation of 
the SmartHarvest system, the researchers propose the 
following recommendations: 
1. The administrators should clearly define and 
enforce the RSBSA number verification process. 
This will actively support accurate and secure 
agricultural data reporting within the system.  
2.  It is recommended to establish partnerships with 
local agricultural cooperatives located in Benguet. This 
strategic partnership will help expand system access and 
build platform credibility among the architectural user 
base.  
3.  Developers should enable offline access to the 
planting schedule for recommendations. Adding this 
feature will significantly increase usability for farmers 
working in rural areas that suffer from low connectivity. 
4. The developers should conduct regular usability 
testing with actual farmers. Continuous evaluation will 
help identify specific pain points and further optimize 
the user experience in future updates.

Chapter 1 
INTRODUCTION 
Background of the Study 
The agricultural sector serves as the backbone of many 
national economies, yet it remains highly vulnerable to the 
increasing unpredictability of weather patterns. This 
creates a significant challenge for farmers: accurately 
predicting crop yields. In their study on this issue, 
Kalpana et al. (2023) highlight that Indian farmers have 
traditionally relied on yield data from previous years to 
make forecasts, a method that is no longer reliable in the 
face of modern climate change. This problem is made worse 
by a lack of knowledge among modern farmers about the best 
time to plant, leading to poor resource use and food 
insecurity. The study suggests using modern data mining and 
machine learning methods, like the Random Forest algorithm, 
to analyze agricultural data and improve yield prediction. 
For agriculture to survive, farming must shift from 
guesswork to data-driven decision-making. Quitaleg and 
Dumlao (2025) emphasize that analyzing past crop production 
is the key to predicting future trends, especially for 
highland vegetables. Understanding how historical yield 
reacts to specific weather patterns allows farmers to 

===== PDF PAGE 14 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 14 
 
 
 
 
anticipate risks and reduce their losses. Instead of 
relying on trial and error, looking at historical data 
helps farmers make informed decisions about what and when 
to plant to ensure a good harvest. 
Moreover, the challenge of keeping a stable food 
supply is not only a safety concern but also a major 
economic issue. Agriculture is a key part of the economy of 
India, providing 17–18% of its GDP and jobs for more than 
half of its population. Because of this, Sharma et al. 
(2023) note that accurate crop production estimation is 
essential for protecting both food security and financial 
stability. They explain that relying only on past data is 
no longer enough, and modern methods are now crucial. This 
shift is not just about stability but also about increasing 
profit. Farmers today aim to “earn more with fewer 
expenses,” moving away from traditional forecasting based 
on personal experience. Since this old method is often 
ineffective, especially when farmers are “unaware of the 
profits they get after cultivation” and need to choose the 
right crops, new decision-support tools have been 
developed. These systems recommend the “best feasible” and 
most profitable crop for specific environmental conditions 
of Indian farmers (Agarwal & Tarar, 2021). 

===== PDF PAGE 15 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 15 
 
 
 
 
According to Kumari et al. (2025), this situation is 
felt across South Asian countries, where crops like rice 
serve as a vital commodity for the sustenance and 
livelihood of resource-poor farmers. Unfortunately, 
management malpractices have vastly impacted productivity 
in these regions. To counter this, experts emphasize the 
need to improve the adaptive capacity of farmers by 
deploying resource-efficient technologies. These 
innovations are crucial not only for increasing yield but 
also for reducing the cost of cultivation, finally making 
production systems more profitable and sustainable for 
those who depend on them for survival.  
Akther et al. (2024) support this trend in their study 
in Sylhet, Bangladesh—an area with similar agricultural 
challenges. Their research focused on improving the 
limitations of traditional methods and the lack of accurate 
tools. Using the “EcoRiceDynamics (1971–2021)” dataset, 
they tested several machine learning models like Linear 
Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, and Gradient Boosted. 
Their results showed that machine learning can be very 
accurate: the Gradient Boosted model explained about 95% of 
rice yield changes, and the Random Forest model greatly 
reduced prediction errors. Validating this approach for the 

===== PDF PAGE 16 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 16 
 
 
 
 
local context, the researchers conducted two distinct 
comparative analyses to determine the optimal tool for the 
system. 
First, for the Crop Yield Prediction module, Random 
Forest was tested against Linear Regression and Ridge 
Regression, where it successfully achieved the lowest Root 
Mean Squared Error (RMSE), meaning the machine learning 
model is more accurate. Second, for the Seasonal 
Forecasting module, Random Forest Time-Series model was 
evaluated against Time Series forecasting algorithms, 
including ARIMA, Prophet, LSTM, and XGBoost. In both tests, 
the Random Forest consistently outperformed all, which 
shows superior accuracy in handling both the non-linear 
variables of crop growth and the complex seasonal trends of 
the province.  
This national and global problem is felt at the local 
level as well. Across the Philippines, farmers are moving 
away from traditional planting to grow high-value crops in 
hopes of earning a better living. However, this shift 
creates a difficult situation. While farmers try to meet 
modern market demands, they are forced to rely on outdated 
methods that cannot cope with the unpredictable weather 
today. This mismatch between their goals and their tools 

===== PDF PAGE 17 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 17 
 
 
 
 
puts them at serious risk. Without access to modern 
technology, they face financial instability, threatening 
both their livelihoods and the food supply of the country. 
This national problem is felt deeply at the local 
level. In highland areas like Ifugao, farmers are 
converting ancestral rice fields into commercial vegetable 
farms to reach city markets and increase their income. 
Similarly, farmers in Benguet are facing a crisis because 
their traditional ways of observing the weather are no 
longer accurate. Relying on guesswork instead of data leads 
to poor harvests and wasted resources. Importantly, these 
communities are not against using technology. In fact, they 
are eager for modern tools that can help them plan better, 
replace unreliable methods, and finally secure their 
financial future. 
An analysis of the target end users, the farmers of 
Benguet, shows that they are facing serious challenges. 
They often depend on traditional planting methods and 
simple weather observation, which are becoming unreliable 
due to changing weather patterns. This leads to frustration 
and financial stress caused by unpredictable harvests and 
unstable income. As will be explained in Chapter 2, these 
farmers are not against using technology. In fact, they are 

===== PDF PAGE 18 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 18 
 
 
 
 
hopeful for a data-based tool that can help them plan 
better and secure their livelihood. 
The landscape in Ifugao has faced a rapid shift toward 
temperate vegetable production. In this high-altitude 
province, rice farming is primarily productive and 
cultural, as the temperature creates a prime agricultural 
land for high-value vegetables. Farmers have progressively 
given up on traditional rice fields and swidden farms as 
roads have improved and linked these formerly isolated 
communities with urban markets. These areas have been 
converted into commercial "vegetable terraces" to take 
advantage of the higher potential income from agricultural 
land (Albano, 2025). 
The intentional abandonment of youth from the 
agriculture industry causes an aging problem. The 
opportunity cost of labor has changed due to rapid 
urbanization and the growth of the services sector. Young 
Filipinos are choosing to migrate to urban areas or 
overseas labor markets after witnessing their parents' 
physical hardships and financial instability. 
This phenomenon is motivated by rational economic 
behavior; the returns on labor in agriculture are 
substantially lower than in industry or services, and the 

===== PDF PAGE 19 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 19 
 
 
 
 
relationship between education and income is weaker in 
farming than in other sectors. Small farm sizes create a 
structural barrier to efficiency. They limit the 
application of economies of scale, making the per-unit cost 
of mechanization prohibitively high. Collecting produce 
from hundreds of small farms to fill the market requires a 
complex and costly aggregation of infrastructures by 
middlemen that keeps an amount of the value.  
An effective case study of this vulnerability is the 
El Niño event of 2023–2024. Long-lasting dry periods 
brought on by the phenomenon significantly harmed crops 
throughout the region.  
Water stress was predicted to cause a 3.63% decrease 
in unmilled rice (palay) production in 2024. Over 163,000 
hectares of cropland suffered losses of PHP 9.50 billion, 
according to the Department of Agriculture (DA). This 
creates a critical need for low-cost digital interventions 
that can improve efficiency without requiring heavy capital 
investment. By introducing a data-driven Decision Support 
System, the project not only aids the aging workforce in 
making easier, more accurate decisions but also introduces 
a level of technological modernization that is essential 
for retaining the next generation of farmers.

Definition of Terms 
To ensure clarity and consistency throughout this 
study, the following terms are defined as they are used 
within the context of the research and system development. 
These definitions aim to provide a common understanding of 
key concepts and functionalities relevant. 
Agricultural Technologist. Refers to the government 
officers or field agents under the Department of 
Agriculture (DA-CAR) who monitor regional crop performance 
and provide technical assistance to farmers. In this study, 
they are the secondary users of the system who utilize the 
Admin Dashboard for monitoring and reporting. 

===== PDF PAGE 25 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 25 
 
 
 
 
API (Application Programming Interface). A software 
bridge that allows two different applications to talk to 
each other. In this study, it refers to the technology used 
to connect SmartHarvest with external weather providers 
like OpenWeather and Open-Meteo to fetch real-time climate 
data automatically. 
Data Mining. The process of analyzing large datasets 
to discover hidden patterns and relationships. In this 
research, it refers to the technique used to extract 
insights from historical agricultural records and climate 
data to forecast future trends. 
Decision Support System (DSS). A computerized system 
designed to aid in determining judgments and courses of 
action. In this study, it refers to "SmartHarvest," the 
web-based platform that analyzes data to provide farmers 
with recommendations on planting schedules and crop 
selection. 
Food Security. The state of having reliable access to 
affordable, nutritious food. This is identified as a 
primary economic and safety concern in India and the 
Philippines that the proposed system aims to address by 
stabilizing crop production. 

===== PDF PAGE 26 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 26 
 
 
 
 
Internet of Things (IoT). A network of physical 
objects that are embedded with sensors, software, and other 
technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging 
data with other devices and systems over the internet. In 
this study, it refers to the hardware component of the 
system, specifically the interconnected sensors and 
microcontrollers that automate irrigation to crops. 
Machine Learning. A subset of artificial intelligence 
that enables systems to learn from data and improve from 
experience without being explicitly programmed. In this 
study, it refers to the computational approach used to 
train the system to predict crop yields based on 
environmental variables. 
Planting Window. The ideal time frame recommended by 
the system for planting a specific crop to ensure the 
highest probability of a good harvest based on weather 
forecasts. 
Random Forest Algorithm. A machine learning technique 
that operates by constructing a multitude of decision trees 
during training. In this study, it is the specific 
algorithm chosen for the "Predictive Engine" because it 
demonstrated the lowest Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and 

===== PDF PAGE 27 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 27 
 
 
 
 
superior accuracy in handling non-linear variables compared 
to other models like Linear Regression. 
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). A security mechanism 
that restricts system access based on the roles of 
individual users. 
Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE). A standard statistical 
metric used to measure the accuracy of the predictions of a 
model. In this research, it is the benchmark used to 
validate that the Random Forest model is the most effective 
tool for crop yield prediction. 
SmartHarvest. The specific name of the decision 
support system developed in this capstone project. It is a 
web-based tool designed to help Benguet farmers optimize 
planting schedules using historical yield and climate 
patterns. 
UI/UX (User Interface/User Experience). Refers to the 
design of the system in terms of looks (UI) and how easy it 
is to use (UX). In this study, the goal was to create a 
"farmer-friendly" UI/UX that makes complex data easy to 
understand for non-technical users. 
Proposed Solution 
In the agricultural sector of Benguet, decision-making 
has traditionally relied on generational knowledge and 

===== PDF PAGE 28 =====

 
 
 INTRODUCTION 28 
 
 
 
 
generalized weather forecasts. These traditional methods 
often struggle to take accountability for rapid climate 
variability and complex market dynamics, leading to 
inefficiencies in production and potential economic loss. 
This study proposes the development of a decision support 
system that leverages historical data and advanced 
computational techniques. By integrating modern web 
technologies with data analytics, the researchers aim to 
transform how agricultural planning is approached, moving 
from reactive measures to predictive, data-driven 
strategies. 
SmartHarvest is designed to be an accessible and 
responsive web-based system that provides clear, timely,

 The researchers adopted the Agile Software Development 
Methodology to build SmartHarvest: A Decision Support System for 
Optimizing Planting Schedule Based on Historical Yield and 
Climate Patterns. According to Guerrero-Ulloa et al. (2023), 
adopting an iterative methodology, specifically agile 
methodology, allows developers to continuously refine the 
architecture of the system through repeated testing cycles, 
ensuring that components such as predictive accuracy and user 
interface meet the standards required for deployment. 
 The researchers selected agile methodology because of the 
complexity of the project requirement to integrate a dynamic 
Machine Learning model with a user-friendly web interface. 
Developing a predictive engine using the Random Forest algorithm 
requires constant tuning and retraining to achieve high 
accuracy. The Agile framework allows the researchers to revisit 
and improve the algorithm without disrupting the development of 
the web platform, ensuring that the final system is both

technically healthy and aligned with the practical needs of the 
farmers in Benguet and officers of the DA-CAR.

Agriculture (RSBSA) number. Upon registration, farmers are 
required to provide their name, email, contact number, and 
location details, which are used to identify and authenticate 
them within the system. 
To cater to user accessibility and varying technical 
backgrounds in the field, farmers are provided with two distinct 
options to log into their accounts, they may use either their 
registered email address or the unique RSBSA number officially 
issued to them by the DA CAR. Providing the RSBSA number as an 
alternative login credential specifically accommodates farmers 
with lower digital literacy who may face challenges managing 
traditional email accounts, thereby ensuring inclusive access to 
the platform without technical barriers. A unique, valid RSBSA 
number remains a strict prerequisite for farmers wishing to 
access the system's core functional modules. This mechanism 
ensures that their identity as a registered agricultural worker 
is authenticated, which helps prevent fraudulent reporting, 
facilitates government data matching, and maintains overall 
system integrity. To ensure security and data validity within 
the platform, the current registration workflow mandates that 

===== PDF PAGE 68 =====

 
 
 
all newly registered farmer accounts must be manually reviewed 
and verified by a DA Administrator before full access is 
granted. 
On the other hand, the Department of Agriculture (DA) 
Administrator information consists of the administrator's name, 
official email address, and phone number. Once registered and 
verified, both farmers and DA administrators can access their 
respective dashboards, where they can manage their profiles, 
track active agricultural cycles, manage market prices and 
others. 
Lastly, the Super Administrator information comprises the 
super admin’s account credentials and a mandatory Two-Factor 
Authentication (2FA) security key. The Super Administrator holds 
top-tier system privileges, fundamentally responsible for system 
configurations and institutional management, which includes 
provisioning and adding authorized personnel into the system 
under the DA Administrator role. Due to the high-level system 
permissions attached to this role, the Super Administrator 
cannot bypass security with a standard password. The system 
strictly mandates that the Super Admin can only log in by 
verifying their identity through a time-based 2FA authenticator 
application.

This comprehensive multi-perspective assessment ensures the

According to Brooke. (1996), the System Usability 
Scale (SUS) is a questionnaire to evaluate the usability of 
products and services using a 5-Likert scale ranging from 
"I strongly agree" to "I strongly disagree." It serves as a 
quantitative method to assess the usability of various 
systems. To determine the appropriate respondents for the 
usability testing, the researchers used purposive sampling. 
According to Etikan et al. (2016), purposive sampling is a 
non-probability sampling technique where participants are 
deliberately chosen based on the judgment of the 
researchers or experts, focusing on individuals who possess 
the specific characteristics required for the study. 
In this context, the selection of the 132 farmer 
respondents was directly facilitated by the project’s 
partner organization, the DA-CAR. Rather than using a 
randomized population formula, the researchers relied on 
DA-CAR to identify, contact, and gather the available local 
farmers who best fit the target demographic to accurately 
test and evaluate the SmartHarvest system.  
Moreover, to evaluate the system’s usability from the 
target users' perspectives, 132 purposively selected 
farmers, facilitated through the partnership with DA-CAR, 
participated in the usability testing. The researchers 

===== PDF PAGE 109 =====

 
 
 
distributed printed questionnaires to the respondents 
during in-person testing sessions.  
Upon collecting the printed SUS questionnaire 
responses, each individual response is converted into 
corresponding scores. For the odd-numbered questions (1, 3, 
5, 7, and 9), the number 1 is subtracted from the 
participant’s original response. For the even-numbered 
questions (2, 4, 6, 8, and 10), the participant’s response 
is subtracted from the number 5. 
All of the adjusted scores from the questionnaire are 
then summed together, and this total is subsequently 
multiplied by 2.5 to obtain the final overall SUS score per 
respondent.  
To calculate the average SUS score from the collected 
responses, the following formula is used by the 
researchers: 
Average SUS Score = (Σ SUS Scores) / n 
Where: 
• Σ SUS Scores represents the total sum of the 
individual SUS scores from all participants. 
• n represents the total number of respondents.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
This chapter provides a summary of the conclusions 
drawn from the results and findings of the study. It 
presents the key interpretations which reflect how the 
objectives of the research were addressed. In addition, it 
presents the researchers' recommendations, which are 
intended to guide future researchers and developers who may 
undertake similar studies. 
Conclusions 
1. The information requirements of the system were 
successfully identified and categorized into three main 
parts: farmers, DA administrators, and super 
administrators. Farmers are required to provide full name, 
contact details, and a valid RSBSA number for verification. 
Administrators are required to provide official 
credentials, contact details, and 2FA authentication. 
System requirements such as RAM, storage, OS version, and 
internet connectivity were also specified. 
2. The 4+1 architectural framework, supported by UML 
diagrams, effectively guided the system’s structural design 
and facilitated a smooth transition from planning to 
development. 

===== PDF PAGE 128 =====

 
 
 
3. The study identified core features of the system, 
including Random Forest-based crop schedule forecasting, 
comprehensive yield analysis, agricultural weather 
forecasts, and a provincial monitoring dashboard. 
4. The SmartHarvest system was evaluated by DA Staff 
across four USE dimensions: Usefulness, Ease of Use, Ease 
of Learning, and Satisfaction. The system achieved an 
overall weighted mean of 6.32, corresponding to "Strongly 
Agree" on the Likert Scale. Overall, the admin dashboard 
earned a "Strongly Agree" rating, reflecting highly 
effective usability, while the system placed within the ‘C- 
to C+’ ("OK–Good" / Marginal–Acceptable) grade range 
utilizing the SUS instrument. This indicates that while the 
system is highly effective overall, the system has minor 
areas that can be improved. 
Recommendations 
1. Clearly define and enforce the RSBSA number 
verification process to support accurate and secure 
agricultural data reporting. 
2. Establish partnerships with local agricultural 
cooperatives in Benguet to expand system access and 
build platform credibility. 

===== PDF PAGE 129 =====

 
 
 
3. Enable offline access to the planting schedule 
recommendations to increase usability for farmers in 
low-connectivity rural areas. 
4. Conduct regular usability testing with actual 
farmers to continuously identify pain points and 
optimize user experience in future updates.
