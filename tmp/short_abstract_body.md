### 7.1 Rationale/Background of the Study

Vegetable production is an important agricultural activity in Benguet, but crop plans, damage reports, and harvest records are difficult to monitor when they are handled through separate processes. Farmers need an organized way to manage and submit farm records, while agricultural administrators require updated and validated information for planning. Harviana was developed as a GIS-integrated decision-support system that organizes agricultural records, presents location-based production information, and links Farmers, LGU Validators, and DA Administrators through submission, validation, monitoring, and reporting.

### 7.2 Summary

This study aimed to design and develop Harviana: A GIS-Integrated Decision Support System for Visualizing Vegetable Production Trends in Benguet. Specifically, it aimed to: (1) identify the system’s information requirements; (2) determine its architectural framework; (3) identify its features; and (4) measure its usability among participating Farmers and DA Administrators.

The researchers used Scrum as the specific Agile software development methodology. Development was divided into five Sprints: Requirements Gathering and Planning, GIS Visualization Module Development, Core Feature Development, Machine Learning Production Estimation and Supply Forecast Integration, and Testing and Evaluation. This approach allowed the system components to be developed, reviewed, and refined in manageable stages.

### 7.3 Findings

Based on the data gathered and analyzed in relation to the objectives of the study, the researchers identified the following findings:

1. Harviana requires farmer profile, farm, crop-plan, damage, and actual-harvest information; LGU validation decisions and notes; and consolidated agricultural records for DA monitoring, forecasting, and reporting. Its minimum and recommended system requirements were also identified.

2. The “4+1” View Model described Harviana through its scenario, logical, process, development, and physical views. Harviana is deployed on Railway and connected to its machine-learning service and Railway PostgreSQL.

3. The identified features include crop planning, farmer calendar management, production estimation, damage and actual-harvest reporting, LGU validation, interactive map and weather viewing, municipal supply forecasting, and report generation and export.

4. The 132 Farmer respondents obtained a mean SUS score of 71.55, interpreted as “OK to Good” and “Marginal to Acceptable.” The 18 DA Administrator respondents obtained an overall USE mean of 6.32, interpreted as “Strongly Agree.”

### 7.4. Conclusions

Based on the findings and evaluation of Harviana, the researchers arrived at the following conclusions:

1. The identified information requirements provide the records needed for agricultural monitoring, validation, forecasting, and reporting.

2. The “4+1” View Model provides an organized framework for describing Harviana’s structure, processes, components, and deployment.

3. The system’s features establish a connected workflow from Farmer submission and LGU validation to DA administrative monitoring.

4. Harviana was generally usable for the participating Farmers and DA Administrators, although the farmer-side interface may still be improved for easier first-time use.

### 7.5 Recommendations

Based on the conclusions of the study, the researchers recommend the following:

1. Review the information requirements and validation procedures regularly with the intended users to maintain complete and relevant agricultural records.

2. Maintain and monitor the database, machine-learning service, security controls, backup procedures, and Railway deployment.

3. Future development may support additional crops, more validated production and harvest data, clearer user instructions, and better access in areas with limited internet connectivity.

4. Future usability evaluations should include LGU Validators and respondents from additional municipalities in Benguet to guide further interface and workflow improvements.
